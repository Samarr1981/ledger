# Ledger

A claim-level grounding eval for RAG over insurance policy documents.

## 1. The problem

Production RAG systems report citation coverage: the percentage of answer sentences
carrying a source reference. That number is close to useless. It proves a chunk was
retrieved and attached. It proves nothing about whether the sentence is true relative
to that chunk.

The failure this hides is the dangerous one. An answer that is fluent, fully cited,
and wrong in one clause. A reader who trusts the citation stops checking. A reader who
does check finds the cited passage says something adjacent but different, and loses
trust in the whole system.

In insurance, that clause is a coverage decision.

Ledger measures the thing citation coverage pretends to measure, and reports the gap
between the two.

## 2. Prior art, and what is different here

Claim decomposition plus per-claim verification is not new. It is the standard
definition of RAG faithfulness.

- **RAGAS** (Es et al., 2024) decomposes an answer into statements and checks each
  against the retrieved context. The reference implementation of this approach.
- **DeepEval** follows the same recipe with more emphasis on contradiction detection.
- **ALCE** (Gao et al., 2023) is the dominant citation-quality benchmark, defining
  citation recall and citation precision over cited passages.
- **RAGChecker** adds claim-level decomposition with retrieval-versus-generation
  diagnostics.

Ledger differs on one axis, and the difference is the point of the project:

> RAGAS-style faithfulness verifies each claim against the **entire retrieved context**.
> Ledger verifies each claim against **only the chunks that claim cites**.

Under the standard test, a claim passes if anything in the top-k supports it. The
citation attached to that claim can say something else entirely and the claim still
scores as faithful. Under Ledger's test, it fails. Ledger's test is the one that
matches what a user experiences when they click a citation and read the clause.

This is closer to ALCE citation precision than to RAGAS faithfulness, but applied at
atomic-claim granularity rather than sentence granularity, on a regulated document
corpus that appears in no public benchmark.

Ledger is not a new metric. It is a stricter application of an existing one, on a
corpus where the strictness matters, with human calibration attached.

## 3. Corpus

- `corpus/oap1.pdf` — Ontario Automobile Policy (OAP 1), OAP1-EN.4 (2026), approved
  for use on or after July 1, 2026. 61 pages, Sections 1 to 8, hierarchical subsection
  numbering. Published by FSRA.
- `corpus/opcf20.pdf` — OPCF 20, Coverage for Transportation Replacement (AF-142E).
- `corpus/opcf27.pdf` — OPCF 27, Liability for Damage to Non-Owned Automobile(s)
  (AF-137E).

Chosen because it is the standard contract for every private auto policy in Ontario,
because it is genuinely hard for retrieval (cross-referenced statutory conditions,
exclusions that carve out specific coverages, an endorsement that explicitly overrides
a numbered section of the base document), and because it is a real regulated document
rather than a QA dataset.

The OPCF 20 and OPCF 27 pairing is deliberate. Both concern rental cars. Neither
covers what the other covers. OPCF 20 pays for transportation while your car is out of
service. OPCF 27 covers physical damage to a car you do not own. OAP 1 s.7.4.4 covers
rental after theft, capped, with a waiting period, and OPCF 20 explicitly replaces it.
Three adjacent provisions, three different answers. This is the near-miss test bed.

## 4. Question set

15 minimum, hand-written against the documents, stored in `questions/questions.json`.

```json
{
  "id": "q01",
  "type": "direct | multi_hop | numeric | trap | near_miss",
  "question": "...",
  "notes": "where the answer lives, or that it does not exist in the corpus"
}
```

Target mix: ~5 direct, ~3 multi-hop, ~3 numeric, ~3 trap, ~2 near-miss.

The `notes` field is hand-authored ground truth and is the basis for the Phase 6
verifier agreement check. It must be written by a human reading the source. Notes
generated by a model would make the agreement statistic measure model-model
concordance, which is precisely the objection this project exists to answer.

Trap questions are questions the corpus does not answer. Correct behaviour is refusal.
They are written to sound plausible and stay on-topic, because an obviously
off-domain question tests nothing.

## 5. Pipeline

Six stages. Every stage reads JSON from `runs/` and writes JSON to `runs/`. Stages are
independently re-runnable so a bug in stage 4 does not force re-paying for stages 1
through 3.

### 5.1 Ingest — `src/ingest.py` → `runs/chunks.json`

Parse with pdfplumber. Structure-aware chunking on section boundaries with a maximum
token size. Each chunk carries:

```json
{
  "chunk_id": "c_001",
  "doc": "oap1",
  "section_path": "Section 7 > 7.4 Additional Benefits > 7.4.4 Loss of Use Due to Theft",
  "section_number": "7.4.4",
  "page": 48,
  "parent_section": "7.4",
  "text": "..."
}
```

Exclusions must carry a `parent_section` reference to the coverage they modify. An
exclusion severed from its coverage generates confidently wrong answers, and OAP 1 is
full of them: s.1.8.5 excludes war losses but carves out Liability Coverage; s.7.2.1
through s.7.2.3 apply only to Section 7.

### 5.2 Baseline RAG — `src/rag.py` → `runs/answers.json`

BM25 over chunks via `rank_bm25`, top-5. Generate with claude-sonnet-4-6, temperature 0,
instructed to answer only from the provided chunks and to cite chunk_ids inline as
`[chunk_id]` after each sentence.

```json
{
  "question_id": "q01",
  "answer_text": "...",
  "retrieved_chunk_ids": ["c_112", "c_113"],
  "cited_chunk_ids": ["c_112"]
}
```

Deliberately boring. Top-5 BM25 with a cite-your-sources prompt is representative of
what a large share of production systems actually do. The finding only means something
if the baseline is ordinary rather than sabotaged. Do not improve retrieval here.

### 5.3 Claim decomposition — `src/decompose.py` → `runs/claims.json`

Split each answer into atomic claims. One verifiable assertion per claim. Each claim
inherits the citations attached to the sentence it came from.

```json
{
  "question_id": "q01",
  "claim_id": "q01_c3",
  "claim_text": "...",
  "cited_chunk_ids": ["c_112"]
}
```

Claims must be self-contained. Three failure modes to iterate the prompt against:

- **Bundled claims.** "Coverage exists and the cap is $900" is two assertions.
- **Dangling reference.** "It applies unless the vehicle is stolen" is unverifiable
  in isolation.
- **Meta-commentary.** "The policy discusses loss of use" asserts nothing.

This stage gates everything downstream. Every claim gets read by hand during
development.

### 5.4 Blind verification — `src/verify.py` → `runs/verdicts.json`

**The core methodological rule.** For each claim, a fresh LLM call receives:

- the claim text
- the full text of the chunks that claim cites

and nothing else. Not the question. Not the rest of the answer. Not the other
retrieved chunks.

If the verifier sees the question, it starts assessing whether the answer is
*reasonable* rather than whether this specific claim is *supported by this specific
passage*. That is the difference between an eval and a vibe check.

Verdicts:

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `SUPPORTED` | The cited passage states this, or entails it directly |
| `PARTIAL` | The passage supports part of the claim, or supports it with a qualification the claim omits |
| `UNSUPPORTED` | The passage does not address this claim |
| `CONTRADICTED` | The passage states something incompatible with this claim |

Plus one sentence of reasoning per verdict. The rubric needs one worked example per
verdict in the prompt; the common drift is the verifier being too generous with
PARTIAL where UNSUPPORTED is correct.

### 5.5 Failure attribution — `src/attribute.py` → `runs/attribution.json`

For every claim not `SUPPORTED`, search all chunks (BM25 over the full corpus, then an
LLM check of the top hits) for any chunk that would have supported it.

| Classification | Condition |
|---|---|
| `retrieval_failure` | A supporting chunk exists but was not retrieved |
| `generation_failure` | A supporting chunk exists and was retrieved, but the claim is still unsupported by its citation |
| `fabrication` | No chunk anywhere in the corpus supports the claim |

These have completely different fixes. Most reporting collapses them into one
hallucination rate.

### 5.6 Gate and report — `src/gate.py`, `src/report.py`

Gate: per-answer grounding score is the fraction of that answer's claims verdicted
`SUPPORTED`. Below threshold (default 0.8) the answer becomes `REFUSED`.

Report: one static HTML file, no framework.

## 6. Metrics

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Answer-level citation coverage | Fraction of answer sentences carrying at least one chunk_id |
| Claim-level grounding rate | Fraction of atomic claims verdicted SUPPORTED |
| **The gap** | The difference between the two. The headline. |
| Failure split | retrieval / generation / fabrication, as counts and percentages |
| Trap refusal rate | Fraction of `trap`-type questions where the system declined to answer |
| Gate effect | Answers passing and failing at threshold 0.8 |
| Verifier agreement | Percent agreement with hand labels, plus confusion matrix |

Headline format: *X% of sentences cited, Y% of claims grounded, across N claims from
M questions.* The N and M are stated every time the number is. A grounding rate quoted
without its sample size is marketing.

## 7. Verifier agreement

Hand-label 20 to 30 claims against their cited passages using the `notes` ground truth,
stored in `questions/hand_labels.json` using the same four verdicts. `src/agreement.py`
compares those labels to the verifier's verdicts and reports percent agreement and the
confusion matrix.

This pre-answers the "an LLM grading an LLM proves nothing" objection before it is
raised, and it is the single line that separates this from a weekend of library glue.
Industry guidance repeatedly recommends exactly this manual verification step for
regulated domains. Almost nobody does it.

## 8. Non-goals

Explicitly not building, at any point, for any reason:

- Vector database
- Chat UI, web app, or any interactive frontend
- Authentication, accounts, multi-user anything
- Voice, or any integration with other projects
- Landing page or marketing site
- Embedding-based retrieval (BM25 only; swapping it later is a one-file change)
- Retrieval improvements of any kind on the baseline

Scope creep on this list is the main risk to shipping.

## 9. Definition of done

- One command runs the full pipeline on all questions and produces the HTML report
- The headline gap is stated with its sample size
- Failure split is reported: retrieval / generation / fabrication
- Trap-question refusal rate is reported separately
- Verifier agreement with hand labels is in the report
- The writeup ships at least one failure of Ledger's own pipeline

## 10. Known limitations

Stated up front rather than discovered by a reviewer.

- **Sample size is small.** Fifteen questions yields roughly 50 to 70 claims. This is a
  working harness demonstration, not a benchmark. Results are directional.
- **The verifier is an LLM.** Section 7 quantifies how much that should worry you, but
  it does not eliminate the concern.
- **Claim decomposition is a dependency.** If decomposition over-splits or misses
  claims, every downstream number drifts. Decomposition quality is checked by hand
  during development and remains the weakest link.
- **Single corpus, single domain.** Nothing here establishes that the gap generalises
  beyond Ontario auto policy documents.
- **Single generator model.** The gap is measured for one model at one temperature.
  Comparing generators is a natural extension and is out of scope for v1.
